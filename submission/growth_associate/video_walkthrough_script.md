# Video walkthrough script — 5 to 8 minutes

**Proposed event:** Campus Project & Innovation Showcase 2027, a proposed first edition at `[COLLEGE NAME]`. No event has been organized or recorded for this assignment. The college name and local evidence were not included in the supplied brief, so fill the bracket and cite an official calendar or event page before recording if available.

## 0:00–0:45 — Context and audience

“I’m proposing a one-day Campus Project and Innovation Showcase for students to present practical work across departments. The audience would be student teams, peers, faculty reviewers, and campus support staff. I chose a small first edition to make it manageable: up to 20 teams and a hard cap of 100 people, but those are planning assumptions. I would confirm the college name, venue capacity, and exam calendar before announcing anything. The assignment materials do not include college-specific evidence, so I have left that explicit rather than claiming a past event.”

## 0:45–1:35 — Goal and success criteria

“The goal is for students to explain the problem, their approach, and what they learned from trying a solution. I would measure registrations and checked-in attendance separately, so the attendance rate has a clear denominator. I would also track the share of scheduled teams that present, feedback response rate, average usefulness score, budget variance, and whether the promised deliverables are complete. These are targets for a proposed event, not results. I would reset them if the approved room or college calendar suggests a different scale.”

## 1:35–2:25 — Timeline and date choice

“The date is deliberately a window, not a confirmed day. At eight weeks out, I would check the official academic and exam timetable, two fallback dates, and faculty availability. At seven weeks I would request the room and budget approval. At six weeks I would open a minimal registration form. At five weeks I would confirm teams using transparent criteria. Promotion would start only after the date, room, budget, branding, and privacy language are approved. In the final two weeks I would test each presentation, accessibility need, and equipment fallback.”

## 2:25–3:20 — Budget and resources

“The planning ceiling is 35,200 rupees including a 10 percent contingency. The largest ranges are the venue and equipment because the college may provide those in kind. Printing, small awards, logistics, and optional recording are also estimates, not quotes or approved costs. Before a commitment I would check campus inventory, procurement rules, and local quotes. If the room and AV are provided, I would report them as in-kind support rather than describing them as actual zero-cost purchases.”

## 3:20–4:00 — Promotion and registration

“I would use official department channels, noticeboards, and approved college social accounts. The call for teams and the audience registration are separate. The registration form asks for only the information needed for updates and check-in. We close at the venue-approved capacity, use a waitlist, and offer a paper check-in option. Registration is not consent to appear in a photo or video.”

## 4:00–4:45 — Approvals

“The faculty coordinator approves the concept and faculty supervision. Facilities and administration approve the room, safety, accessibility, and equipment. Finance or the delegated administrator approves the budget and purchasing route. The communications or branding owner checks the poster and college identity. A media or privacy authority approves the consent wording, storage, retention, and any publication. I would keep the written decisions with the event brief and pause any item whose approval is still pending.”

## 4:45–5:40 — Event day and responsibilities

“The operations lead opens the room at 7:30, checks safe cable routes and tests the offline slides. Registration volunteers reconcile QR and paper check-in. The host gives the schedule and privacy notice before the first presentation. A stage manager keeps the slots on time; a separate AV operator handles the screen and microphone; team liaisons help presenters and check accessibility needs. If a live demo is unsafe or the network fails, the fallback is a reviewed slide or poster walkthrough. Entry stops at the approved capacity.”

## 5:40–6:35 — Recording and privacy

“Recording is optional. I would ask for separate opt-in to capture and to publish, create a no-camera seating area, and assign one person to check consent while another operates the camera. The shot list focuses on the event sign, permitted project details, and presenters who opted in. I would avoid attendee badges, private screens, and incidental close-ups. Before publishing, I would check the consent register, remove non-consenting people and private information, review captions, approve the destination, and set a deletion date. If anything is unclear, that clip does not go out.”

## 6:35–7:20 — Review and my proposed contribution

“Within two days I would reconcile registrations, check-ins, teams scheduled and teams who presented, feedback, receipts, incidents, and deliverables. Within a week I would share a short review with denominators and budget variance, plus actions with an owner and due date. I would take the proposed operations and measurement lead role: maintain the run sheet and decision log, coordinate volunteers and check-in, and prepare that review. The faculty coordinator and college approvers would retain the authority to approve the venue, spending, branding, and recording.”

## 7:20–7:35 — Closing and fact check

“The event name, scale, March window, and budget are proposals. The college, exam calendar, venue, costs, and approvals remain to be confirmed. I have used AI to structure and edit the planning materials, but no real event, approval, attendance, video, or result is being represented as completed.”
