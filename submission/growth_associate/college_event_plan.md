## Event and audience

The showcase gives student teams a structured place to present class, club, or independent projects to peers and faculty. The proposed first edition is intentionally small: up to 20 project teams and 100 total attendees, with the capacity cap confirmed against the approved room and fire/safety limits. Invite students across departments, faculty reviewers, and relevant campus support staff. Do not advertise external guests until administration approves.

**Purpose:** help students explain how they framed a problem, built or researched a solution, and learned from testing; make it easier for peers to discover work across departments.

**Proposed success criteria (targets, not results):**

- At least 60 valid registrations, with a venue-based hard cap and a waitlist if needed.
- Attendance rate of at least 70% of confirmed registrations, calculated as checked-in attendees / confirmed registrations.
- At least 80% of registered teams complete their scheduled presentation or demo.
- Feedback response from at least half of checked-in attendees; mean usefulness score at least 4/5.
- Final spend within 10% of the approved ceiling, with receipts and variance notes.
- All agreed deliverables completed: run sheet, accessible slides or posters, consent-controlled media, feedback summary, and action log.

Targets should be reset after the college and venue confirm realistic capacity. Do not report any target as an achieved outcome.

## Venue and resources

Request one accessible classroom, seminar room, or hall with capacity for the approved cap; accessible entry and seating; projector/display; microphones if the room requires them; extension leads managed under campus rules; stable power; tables for project demos; and a quiet registration area. The room and equipment are not confirmed. Facilities staff must approve setup, load limits, cable routing, and any high-power or live hardware demonstrations. Keep a no-network demo mode and downloadable slide copy for every team.

Track registrations in a restricted college-approved form or spreadsheet using a minimum set of fields: name or college identifier as policy allows, contact for event updates, department/team, attendee status, accessibility request (optional and restricted), and media consent choice. Use a separate check-in list with limited volunteer access. Avoid public attendee lists. Record attendance once at entry; distinguish registration, confirmed attendance, and team participation.

## Recording, privacy, and publishing plan

1. **Consent:** obtain separate, plain-language opt-in for identifiable video/interviews and for publication. Attendance is not consent. Use a visible recording notice, a no-camera seating/route, and a simple way to opt out on the day. A team may present without being filmed. Get guardian consent if college policy requires it for any participant under 18. Follow the college's retention and withdrawal policy.
2. **Shot list:** exterior/event sign without restricted identifiers; room wide shot before doors; hands-on project details with team permission; presentation screen with approved content; consenting presenter mid-shot; audience reaction only where each identifiable person has opted in; short voluntary post-session reflections. Avoid attendee lists, private screens, badges with personal details, and incidental close-ups.
3. **Capture:** one named operator and one consent checker; use college-approved equipment/storage; log clip ID, time, consent scope, restrictions, and owner. Stop capture immediately on request. Do not record sensitive demos, private messages, or unreleased work without explicit approval.
4. **Edit:** select only in-scope clips; remove incidental non-consenting people, private data, and unsafe footage. Keep factual captions, speaker names only with permission, and no unapproved endorsements. Retain an unedited restricted source copy only for the approved retention period.
5. **Accessibility:** caption all spoken video, check captions manually, provide alt text for stills, and share a text summary. Do not rely on auto-captions without review.
6. **Publish checklist:** faculty/media approval; consent register reconciled; participant names and project IP checked; captions and audio checked; accessible description; destination and audience confirmed; contact/withdrawal route displayed; retention/deletion date recorded. If any consent or approval is unclear, do not publish that clip.

## Post-event review

Report only measured results after reconciliation. Calculate attendance rate = checked-in attendees / confirmed registrations; team completion = teams that presented / teams scheduled; feedback rate = valid feedback responses / checked-in attendees; average usefulness = mean valid 1–5 responses; budget variance = (actual spend − approved budget) / approved budget. Report denominator and missing counts for every measure. Summarise session completion, engagement (questions, demo participation, or survey responses with a defined measure), deliverables completed, incidents, accessibility feedback, and actions with an owner and due date. Compare actuals with the proposed targets, but do not call an estimate an outcome.
